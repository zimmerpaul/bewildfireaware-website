PSICC North Pike FDRA — ERC & Largest Fires on Record

Energy Release Component vs. daily fire growth for the largest fires on record in the PSICC North Pike FDRA (the pocket card for this FDRA charts no individual fires) — Rocky Flats (2006), Indian Gulch (2011), Bear Creek (2021), the three largest fires by final acres inside the FDRA polygon in federal records, 2005–2024. Daily fire sizes come from ICS-209 incident situation reports; daily ERC is the FEMS station record for the FDRA's SIG — Bailey (52001), Cheesman (53102), Jones Hill (53004) — fuel model Y — Timber, averaged across stations, 2005–2024.

What it shows

Only one fire here supports day-by-day ERC comparison. Indian Gulch (2011) is the single fire in this FDRA with a usable daily growth timeline paired with station ERC, so no pooled statistics are shown. Its 3 significant growth days averaged the 52nd percentile of the FDRA's Mar–Oct ERC record, versus the 54th on its other active days.

The biggest runs cluster on high-ERC days. Indian Gulch's biggest run (+850 ac on Mar 21, 2011) came at the 54th percentile of ERC.

Within-fire, day-to-day correlation varies (Indian Gulch ρ = 0.10). ERC measures fuel dryness, not wind — the day-to-day trigger for the largest runs is often wind on top of dry fuels, which is worth remembering because wind is not part of the ERC calculation. This is a small, size-selected sample — the 3 largest fires on record in this FDRA (the pocket card for this FDRA charts no individual fires) — so these numbers describe these incidents, not a general model for PSICC North Pike FDRA.

Method & caveats

Fire growth. Cumulative acreage per ICS-209 incident situation report (ICS-209-PLUS v2.0 research dataset, 1999–2020); Bear Creek: rough timeline assembled from WFIGS and news reports, not daily sitreps. Where reports skip days, the acreage gain is spread evenly across the gap. Final acreages shown match the fire-matching manifest: Rocky Flats 1,600; Indian Gulch 1,570; Bear Creek 537.

ERC. FEMS daily NFDR summaries (fuel model Y — Timber) for the FDRA's stations — Bailey (52001), Cheesman (53102), Jones Hill (53004) — downloaded from the FEMS climatology API. SIG daily ERC is the mean of stations reporting that day. Unlike the earlier UCR Middle Elevation report, which had to substitute gridMET ERC-G, this report uses station ERC on the FDRA's own fuel-model scale, so thresholds here are the FDRA's own percentiles: Mar–Oct 2005–2024 90th percentile = 46.9, 97th = 51.2 (4,900 station-mean days). FEMS returns no NFDR records for these stations before 2005-01-01 (1999–2004 queried, empty), so pre-2005 fires appear without ERC.

Statistics. “Significant growth day” = daily gain ≥2% of final fire size (min 100 ac). ρ is Spearman rank correlation between daily ERC and daily growth over each fire's active days; group differences (significant vs. other days) use a Mann–Whitney U normal approximation. Fires with fewer than 4 situation reports, stub timelines, or no station ERC for their year are shown for context and excluded from pooled statistics. This is a small, non-random sample of memorable fires — results describe these incidents, not a general model.

Sources: ICS-209-PLUS v2.0 (figshare 19858927; Nature Sci. Data 2023) · FEMS (fems.fs2c.usda.gov) NFDR daily summaries · NWCG pocket cards · WFIGS and contemporary news coverage where noted. Generated July 2026 for bewildfireaware.com.
